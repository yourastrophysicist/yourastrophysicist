00 · about

Hey, I'm Jessica. Nice to meet you!

Most of my time in Padova is not spent looking at the night sky through a romantic telescope. It is spent at a desk, wrestling with Einstein's field equations, taking apart radiative transfer integrals, and tidying Christoffel symbols on scratch paper.

Before Padova I studied Physics at Universitas Gadjah Mada. My published research follows 33 years of VLBI observations of the quasar 4C31.61, to test how stable it is as an anchor of the celestial reference frame (arXiv:2401.12325).

My favourite planet is WASP-12b. Its whole year lasts about a day, and its star is slowly swallowing it. This year I watch it dip in front of that star, in the lab and in my own code.

Outside the lab I write essays on Substack, mostly in Indonesian, and I make videos as Observationally Speaking.

06 · questions people ask me

FAQ

What do you actually work on?

Observational astrophysics and cosmology. Right now that means exoplanet transits, Bayesian statistics and atmospheric retrievals. Before that it was quasar positions measured with VLBI.

What is Your AstroNotes?

My second brain from the first year of the Padova M.Sc.: more than 1,100 linked notes, a map for each of the ten courses, and cosmology derivations written so that no step is skipped. It lives at yourastro.space/notes.

Why is there a quiz in front of the notes?

It is a game, not a lock. The quiz only lives in the terminal. The notes are public at /notes for anyone.

Can I use your notes for my own studying?

Please do, that is why they are open. If you want to reuse them somewhere else, write to me first.

Do you use AI?

Yes, every day. Claude Code turns my lecture transcripts into notes and checks them against the slides. The rules are strict: anything the lecturer did not say is labelled as an addition, unsure numbers are marked to verify, and there is no result without a calculation or a source. The studying stays mine. I wrote about it in The Telescope Also Has a Selection Function.

Why do you write in Indonesian?

Because the people at home deserve astrophysics in their own language too. Some essays are in English, most are in Indonesian.

What does BRAT mean here?

Loud, curious, a little stubborn, and happy to ask the question nobody else in the room asks.
